What is a text match cut?

A text match cut is a quick sequence of cuts where one word stays locked in the same spot, at the same size, while everything around it changes. Each frame pulls the word out of a different sentence, so your eye stays fixed on the word while the context flickers past. It is a popular trick in short-form video, essay videos and kinetic typography, because it makes a single word feel loud and memorable without any complicated editing.

How it works

  1. Type your word. Short words (up to 23 characters) hit hardest. The preview updates as you type.
  2. Choose a shape. Pick 9:16 for Shorts, Reels or TikTok, 1:1 for feeds, or 16:9 for YouTube and presentations.
  3. Tune the feel. Cuts per second sets the rhythm (8 to 12 feels punchy), zoom controls how big the text is, blur softens the surrounding lines, and duration sets the length. Choose a sound effect or none.
  4. Generate and download. The video is rendered frame by frame in your browser. When it finishes, watch it back, then download it. Not happy? Press Regenerate for a fresh mix of frames.

Everything happens on your device. Your word and your video are never uploaded.

Ideas and use cases

  • Hook for a Short or Reel. Open with the key word of your video for two to three seconds, then cut to the main content. If your video is about "trust", generate it with the word "trust".
  • Intro or title sting. Use your channel's keyword or the episode topic as a one-second opener before the first scene.
  • Section transition in essay videos. Drop a short clip between chapters, with the next chapter's key word as the match cut.
  • Emphasis for quotes and lyrics. Highlight the one word you want viewers to remember, then reveal the full line.
  • Teasers for podcasts and talks. Pair a clip of the key term with a short audio snippet from the episode.
  • Classroom and presentations. Introduce a new term with a three-second clip before you explain it.

In any editor (CapCut, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve), drop the downloaded file on your timeline like a normal clip. Cut your voice-over or music over it, and mute the built-in sound if you want to use your own.

Frequently asked questions

Is my word or my video uploaded anywhere?

No. The word you type and the video you generate are processed entirely inside your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, so there is no account, no queue and no copy of your video on our side.

What file format do I get?

MP4 (H.264 video with AAC sound) in modern browsers. Very old browsers fall back to a live recording that may be WebM. If an editor won't accept WebM, convert it with a free tool such as HandBrake or CapCut.

Which browsers and devices work?

A recent version of Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox on desktop works best. Phones work too, but rendering a 1080 by 1920 video is heavy, so older phones may be slow. A laptop gives the smoothest result.

How long does it take to generate a video?

Usually a few seconds. Frames are rendered one by one and encoded with your browser's built-in video encoder, so the speed depends on your device. Keep the tab open until it finishes. Browsers without that encoder fall back to a live recording, which takes as long as the video itself.

What resolution is the output?

1080 pixels on the short side: 1080 by 1920 for 9:16, 1080 by 1080 for 1:1 and 1920 by 1080 for 16:9. Pick 9:16 for Shorts, Reels and TikTok.

Can I use the videos commercially?

Yes. The videos you create are yours to use in your own content, including monetized channels. The sentences in the frames are original text written for this tool.

Can I write my own sentences for the frames?

Not at the moment. Each frame uses a built-in set of original sentences, mixed randomly. Press Regenerate to get a different mix with the same word.

Does it work with words in other languages?

Yes, but the frames use a serif font, and scripts it doesn't cover fall back to your device's default font, so the look can vary. Latin-script words look the most consistent.

The video looks choppy. What can I do?

Close other tabs and heavy apps, try a shorter duration, or use 1:1 instead of 9:16. Lowering the blur also makes each frame lighter to draw.
